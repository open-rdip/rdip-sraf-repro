Codebook, version 1

The one question you are answering

Is this item actually supported by this paper or by its repository?

MarkMeaning
KKeep. The item is supported by the paper or the repository.
DDiscard. Not supported, or the value is wrong, or it belongs to other work.
UUnsure. You looked and genuinely cannot tell.

Three rules that decide most rows

  • Either source counts. If the paper is silent but requirements.txt declares it, that is K. If the repository is silent but the paper states it, that is K.
  • Judge support, not importance. A detail can be trivial and still be K. It is not your job to decide whether the item matters.
  • U is a real answer. It is more useful than a guess. Many U marks on one field tells us the definition needs work, which is what this round is for.

Field by field

Where to look, in order

  1. The paper's experimental setup or implementation section, and the appendix.
  2. The repository README.
  3. Dependency files: requirements.txt, environment.yml, setup.py, Dockerfile.
  4. Config files: configs/, *.yaml, *.json.
  5. Entry points and their argument parsers: train.py, main.py, run.sh.

Five minutes of searching is enough. If you have not found it by then it is U, or D for seeds.

Three things that matter Work independently. Please do not ask the author which answer is right, and do not open any ground-truth or gold-standard file you come across. The items are deliberately mixed from several sources so you cannot tell which came from where. Do not revise earlier studies after your understanding improves. Leave them and mention it. Drift between early and late studies is something we want to see. Pace. Twenty to thirty minutes per study. Work in sessions of about an hour, because this gets tiring and tired annotation is noisy annotation.

Calibration

Ten codebook items

Each item below comes with the evidence you would have found. Decide K, D or U from the codebook alone. The codebook's own answer and the reason for it appear as soon as you answer, and your first answer is what gets recorded.

These ten items are training, not a test. There is no pass mark and nothing is blocked by how you do: work all ten, read each explanation, and then start. Your first answers are kept and reported in the paper as a calibration figure, because it shows where the codebook and a careful reader of it first diverged. That is the useful measurement here, and it is about our wording at least as much as about your reading.

Second task

The failure taxonomy

Twenty-six repositories built successfully, then an attempt to re-run the experiment and reproduce a number the paper reports failed. Each row gives you the reason our harness recorded. Place each failure on two axes. About two hours.

Axis 1, locus: whose problem is it?

Axis 2, remedy: who could remove it?

Where the boundaries sit artifact against claim. Could a different release by the authors have made it reproducible? If yes, artifact. If nothing they published would help, claim. harness against scope. Could we have fixed it by improving the harness inside the envelope we declared? If yes, harness. If the study is simply bigger than what we said we would attempt, scope. harness against artifact. Who should have supplied the missing piece? The authors, artifact. Our environment regardless, harness.
